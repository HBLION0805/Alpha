## Existing knowledge to enrich

- **Macro thesis and exits:** Distinguish `PRICE_ADVERSE_BUT_THESIS_NOT_INVALIDATED`
  from `THESIS_CORE_VARIABLE_CHANGED` as educational terms only. Check newly
  verified changes to the original thesis variable, such as war, sanctions,
  energy, central-bank policy, rates, dollar or liquidity. A loss alone does not
  disprove the thesis. A compelling story cannot override independent price,
  time, capital or contract exits; a hedge or addition cannot silently rewrite a
  broken thesis. A planned stop is a cost of doing business, and remaining
  value/risk matters more than return to the purchase price. Hard exits take
  priority over proving the original view correct. No new stop percentage.
- **Adding and restructuring:** Repeated rescue additions are not justified by
  sunk cost. At most one planned, disciplined loss addition remains
  `CANDIDATE_UNTESTED`, never an active allowance. Ask: “If I had no position
  today and were given fresh cash, would I buy this exact exposure now?” For an
  option, recheck thesis, time, strike, expiry, premium, combined exposure and
  costs under the authoritative allocation policy. An 80% decline and a later
  35% rise are illustrations, not triggers; theta, IV and expiry can still make
  a directionally correct option unprofitable. A roll realizes/closes the old
  position and opens new exposure. A debit spread changes the payoff structure.
  Neither restructuring is automatic.
- **Hedging:** A hedge may reduce a specified portfolio sensitivity and buy
  decision time during uncertainty. Specify instrument, intended risk, horizon,
  ratio/quantity, cost, basis risk, remaining net exposure and hedge exit.
  Opposite direction alone is not effective protection; both legs can lose. A
  hedge does not repair an invalid thesis. No SPY/QQQ/sector strategy is enabled.
- **Confidence and capital survival:** Recent wins can lead to confidence,
  larger risk and a single loss that gives back several gains. Ask whether one
  ordinary loss at the proposed size would erase a disproportionate share of
  those gains. Capital policy, maximum acceptable loss, contract economics and
  independent evidence govern sizing. Reducing size after a winning streak may
  be considered as a process choice, never an automatic rule.
- **Horizon and contract fit:** Investors may do deeper upfront research and
  reassess periodically; traders may need frequent relevant updates and an
  overnight review. Neither style is inherently superior, constant monitoring
  does not create certainty, and a long business horizon cannot justify holding
  an expiring option indefinitely. The Owner's call/time and put/realistic-price
  emphasis is a heuristic: both sides require direction, magnitude, time,
  strike, expiry, IV, premium and liquidity. A six-month thesis does not by itself
  fit a three-week call; a $100-to-$90 bearish thesis does not by itself justify
  a cheap $60 put. Any intentionally lottery-like far-OTM exposure must be
  labeled and separately risk-bounded. Do not assume markets always rise.
- **Whole-system review:** `psych-tunnel-vision` asks the Owner to read an entry
  signal alongside thesis, counterevidence, invalidation, size, hedge, required
  data, no-chase, reduction and no-trade conditions. Another trader's entry or a
  macro narrative is not independent evidence. It does not diagnose selective
  reading.
- **Short weakness hypothesis:** `psych-short-weakness` records that a 100–500%
  prior gain alone is insufficient to short. Potential future evidence includes
  waning momentum/buying pressure, failed continuation, deteriorating narrative
  or fundamentals, observable capital withdrawal and squeeze/borrow/option risk.
  This is an untested hypothesis, not a stock scanner, shorting rule, portfolio
  hedge claim or expansion beyond GLD/IBIT.

Military sayings about preserving forces remain illustrations, never evidence or
signals. Keep the existing seventeen bias questions; the new batch does not
establish a nonduplicative bias category.

## Three optional prompts

| ID | Stage | Reflection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `entry-size-confidence` | Before entry | Is size supported by the current risk budget and evidence, or by confidence after recent wins? |
| `holding-thesis-change` | While holding | Is price adverse while the original thesis stands, or has a core variable changed? What contemporaneous evidence distinguishes them? |
| `holding-fresh-cash` | While holding | With no current position and fresh cash, would this exposure, strike and expiry still be chosen? If not, why add? |
